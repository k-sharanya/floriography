The Peony

Prosperity Romance Honor Bashfulness Compassion
"Like the peony that opens and opens and opens, this is how I want to meet life—surviving the cold then returning to bloom again."

The Meaning

The peony is a flower of magnificent excess: layer upon layer of petals, a bloom so full it seems almost embarrassed by its own beauty. In the language of flowers, it carries prosperity, romance, and honor, but also a charming older meaning: bashfulness, as though such extravagant beauty must surely be accompanied by a blush.

In East Asian traditions, the peony is the "king of flowers", a symbol of wealth, good fortune, and nobility.

Red peonies speak of passionate love.

White peonies carry regret and apology, or alternatively a shy, modest beauty.

Pink peonies are the most romantic, used for weddings and celebrations of love.

Purple peonies signal nobility and respect.

Mythology & History

The peony's name comes from Paeon, a physician to the Greek gods who used the plant medicinally. According to myth, his teacher Asclepius grew jealous of his skill, and Zeus saved Paeon from his wrath by transforming him into the flower that bore his name. From its very origins, the peony was associated with healing and divine favor.

In China, peonies have been cultivated for over 2,000 years, first as medicine and then as objects of extraordinary beauty. Tang dynasty poets wrote of them obsessively; Empress Wu Zetian reportedly commanded peonies to bloom in her garden in winter, and they obeyed. The city of Luoyang holds a peony festival to this day. In Japan, the peony represents bravery, honor, and good fortune, which is a fitting companion to the samurai tradition.

At a Glance

  • FamilyPaeoniaceae
  • OriginAsia, Southern Europe, and Western North America
  • Bloom SeasonLate spring to early summer (May-June); blooms are brief but spectacular
  • LongevityA well-planted peony can live and bloom for over 100 years
  • ScentRanges from light and rosy to rich and spicy depending on variety
  • Fun FactPeony buds are often covered in ants, which help open the blooms by eating a protective resin

When to Give

Peonies are the great wedding flower: lush, romantic, and abundant. They speak of a prosperous future and a love that is full to overflowing. A bouquet of pink peonies says everything a wedding day needs to say without a single word.

Give peonies to mark moments of genuine celebration: promotions, anniversaries, new homes, new chapters. They are also the most appropriate flower for someone who simply deserves to be surrounded by extraordinary beauty on an ordinary day, which is a reason that needs no occasion at all.
